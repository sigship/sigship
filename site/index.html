Ready to leave port, or not yet.

Ships raise a flag before they leave port. Software never did.

In harbour, flag P, the Blue Peter, tells everyone the vessel is about to sail. In Unix, a signal tells a running program something it has to act on. SIGSHIP is both: one clear message that this app is ready to leave port, or that it isn’t yet.

It runs where the work happens.

Your terminal

Check any project, then paste the fixes into Lovable, Cursor, Bolt or Claude Code as one prompt.

npx sigship --prompt
Your browser

Prefer buttons to a terminal? Open a dashboard with the verdict, every finding and the fix prompt. It runs on your computer and uploads nothing.

npx sigship ui

Add a GitHub check that fails when a change brings a serious problem back.

npx sigship init
Your coding agent

Give your agent the check over MCP, with one instruction: don’t say it’s done while the verdict is NO-GO.

17 checks, chosen for how AI-built apps actually fail.

SEV-1

Blocks launch. Exposes data or money.

  • A live secret is sitting in your codeSomeone reads it from your repo or your JavaScript bundle, and the bill or the data breach arrives before you do.
  • Your .env file is committedEvery secret in that file is now in your git history, including every fork and clone of it.
  • A server secret has a browser-visible nameYour build tool copies it into the JavaScript every visitor downloads, where anyone can read it.
  • Your AI key runs in the browserA stranger copies your key from the network tab and spends your credit on their own project.
  • A database table has no row level securityAnyone with your public Supabase key can read this table. That key ships in your JavaScript.
  • A database policy lets anyone writeRow level security is on, but this policy says yes to everyone, so a stranger can edit or delete your data.
  • Your Stripe webhook accepts anythingSomeone posts a fake "payment succeeded" event to your endpoint and gets your paid plan for free.
  • User input may be pasted into a SQL querySomeone types a crafted value into a form and your database runs it as a command.

SEV-2

Blocks launch. Abuse is easy.

  • Your AI route has no spending brakeOne script loops on this endpoint overnight and you wake up to the invoice.
  • Admin access is decided in the browserSomeone opens dev tools, changes one value, and becomes an admin.
  • Raw HTML goes straight onto the pageA comment or profile field carries a script, and it runs in the browser of every visitor who sees it.

SEV-3

Reported. Blocks only with --strict.

  • Any website can call your APIAnother site calls your endpoints from its own pages, and you never see it in your logs as a problem.
  • A secret may be going to your logsPasswords or tokens end up in a log service that more people can read than your database.
  • One crash takes down the whole screenA single bad record gives your users a white page, and nobody tells you.
  • You will hear about errors from your usersYour app fails for a week before someone is annoyed enough to email you.
  • Nothing checks that a change did not break loginA small fix goes live on Friday and signup is broken until Monday.
  • Your deploys are not repeatableA dependency updates itself on the next deploy and production breaks with no code change.

What it can’t do.

  • It uses pattern checks, so it can miss real problems and flag safe code.
  • It reads your repo, so it can’t see settings you changed only in a dashboard.
  • A GO means these checks found nothing that blocks launch. It doesn’t prove your app is safe.
