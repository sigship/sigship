The flags, and what they mean at sea.

Q · Quebec

"My vessel is healthy and I request free pratique": permission to enter port. A ship flies it to say it has nothing to hide. Cleared.

C · Charlie

"Yes" or "affirmative". Flown by a ship that has been asked a question and can answer it. Verified.

H · Hotel

"I have a pilot on board": a local expert is guiding the ship through tricky water. Pilot on board.

Rules of the flag.

  • Use a flag only for the app that earned it, at the version that earned it.
  • Take the Cleared flag down if a later check says NO-GO, until it says GO again.
  • Never describe a flag as a certification, an audit or a guarantee. It's a check, and we say so.
  • The flag images are ours; use them as they are, unchanged. See the brand page.

Questions

Is a flag a security certificate?

No. A GO means SIGSHIP's checks found nothing that blocks launch. It does not prove the app is safe. A flag says the owner checked before launching, nothing more, and the flag pages say so.

Can anyone add the Cleared flag?

Yes. It is self-declared, like a licence badge. That is exactly why Verified exists: it is drawn by SIGSHIP from a real nightly check, so it cannot be added by hand.

What happens if a Verified app fails a check?

The flag comes down by itself and the page shows the date of the last pass. It goes back up when a nightly check is clean again.

Does a flag expire?

Cleared shows the version that checked the app, so readers can see how old it is. Verified shows the date of the last pass. A pilot review flag shows the date it was signed.
